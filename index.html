04 / LEARN

Small rules. Natural writing.

SAWT records speech sounds with a consonant skeleton and vowels attached at their actual position. The word itself should remain recognizable by sound.

01

Hear the word

Start from the pronounced sounds, not the English spelling. The word CAT can begin with a K sound even though it's spelled with C.

CAT → K[AE]T
02

Mark the vowel

Use the actual vertical, caret, arch or slash shape. The tone goes above or below its consonant. A short I slash is half the length of EE.

S[I]T ≠ S[EE]D
03

Initial sounds can float

When a word starts with a vowel, its mark can be written in the space before the first consonant. A centered bullet is optional, not required.

AZAN → [A]Z[AA]N
04

Keep sounds joined

BH uses B', unvoiced TH uses T', and voiced TH uses D'—each as a single letter form. SH and CH use the handwritten drooping-c stroke.

BHARAT → B'[AA]RT
✳
Official vs. estimated

Notebook examples are preserved exactly. The remaining English words use a small built-in pronunciation list or a marked heuristic. The converter does not pretend a guessed word is a confirmed SAWT rule. -MENT and -LY remain unapproved, so no extra glyphs are invented for them.
